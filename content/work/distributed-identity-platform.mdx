## Context

The public-facing portal citizens use to apply for passports and national IDs —
the Ethiopian immigration portal ([immigration.gov.et](https://immigration.gov.et)).
I worked on it end to end: the React/Next.js (TypeScript) front end applicants
interact with, a C#/.NET backend, and Java/Spring Boot microservices on
PostgreSQL exposing secure REST APIs for high-throughput inter-service data
exchange.

## Constraint

A government identity programme is unforgiving: applications must not be lost,
data must be correct, and downtime is visible nationally. On top of correctness,
the system had to sustain high-throughput inter-service traffic, meet
security-standards compliance, and stay up under SLA-bound support across a
cross-border rollout.

## What I built

- The application-portal front end in React/Next.js and TypeScript.
- Backend services in C#/.NET and Java/Spring Boot on PostgreSQL, with secure
  REST APIs for inter-service exchange.
- A secure **Flutter/Dart mobile app for reading passport and ID documents**,
  implementing ICAO authentication and encryption protocols (BAC, PACE/SAC) for
  international identity-verification compliance.
- AWS CI/CD pipelines with Jenkins and Docker — automated build, test and
  deploy — to shorten release cycles and reduce production incidents.

I led deployment across the MEA region alongside PMs, QA, infrastructure teams
and clients, and provided L2/L3 production support and root-cause analysis under
tight SLAs.

## Decisions and trade-offs

**Polyglot services over a single runtime.** The system runs a React/Next.js
front end, C#/.NET services and Java/Spring Boot microservices rather than one
stack. The cost is real — two backend runtimes, two build and dependency
toolchains, two sets of operational knowledge — but it lets each service use the
platform best suited to it and the team's existing expertise, integrated over
well-defined REST contracts instead of a risky rewrite. The discipline that
makes it hold together is keeping those inter-service contracts stable and
versioned.

**On-device document verification.** The passport/ID reader performs ICAO
authentication — BAC, and PACE/SAC where the chip supports it — on the device,
deriving keys from the MRZ and running secure messaging against the chip locally,
rather than shipping document data to a server to verify. That keeps sensitive
identity data off the network and lets verification work in constrained
conditions, at the cost of implementing the cryptography correctly on the client
where mistakes are harder to patch after release.

## Outcome

Deployed to production across the MEA region and supported under SLA with L2/L3
on-call and root-cause analysis. Moving build, test and deploy onto Jenkins/Docker
pipelines took release off manual steps, shortening cycles and cutting the room
for deployment error.
